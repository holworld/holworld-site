His story

A village, painted for fifty years

Vaikuntam grew up in a village in Karimnagar, where his father kept a grocery shop. As a boy he watched the travelling theatre troupes, in which men played the women's parts with painted faces, heavy jewellery and bright saris. Those women never left him.

He trained in Hyderabad, then won a Lalit Kala Akademi fellowship to study at Baroda under K. G. Subramanyan, who had himself learned from Nandalal Bose, Benode Behari Mukherjee and Ramkinkar Baij at Santiniketan. Subramanyan told him to stop looking at European painting and find what was his own.

What he found was home: the women and men of Telangana, drawn with a strong charcoal line and filled with pure reds, yellows and greens that he refuses to mix. A vermilion bindi, a nose ring, hair pinned back, a sari from the Sircilla looms. Simple details that turn a village woman into an icon.

He has carried the same figures into bronze, painting the metal so the face keeps its colour, and he designed the look of the 1988 Telugu film Daasi. He lives and works in Hyderabad.
