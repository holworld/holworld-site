His story

The line that stayed Indian

Almelkar trained at the Sir J. J. School of Art in Bombay, winning prizes while still a student, and at first painted like the moderns he admired, N. S. Bendre and Walter Langhammer.

Then he turned the other way, back to the miniature, to folk and tribal painting and the borders of textiles. He built a style of fine, sure line and jewel-like colour, with every inch of the surface alive with ornament, and the Lalit Kala Akademi gave him its National Award twice.

In 1968 he went back to the J. J. School as a teacher. His work travelled to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia and Ceylon.

Where he stands

Between Bengal and Bombay

He brought the decorative grace of the Bengal School to the J. J. School's tradition.
