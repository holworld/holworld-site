His story

The line that stayed Indian

Almelkar liked to say he learned to paint before he was born, while his mother sat embroidering all through her pregnancy. He was painting by the age of seven.

He trained at the Sir J. J. School of Art in Bombay, winning prizes from the Bombay Art Society and the Art Society of India, and graduated in 1948. His early work looks towards N. S. Bendre and Walter Langhammer, the modern painters he admired.

Then he turned the other way. He went back to the miniature, to folk and tribal painting and the patterned borders of textiles, and built a style of his own: a fine, sure line, jewel-like colour, and every inch of the surface alive with ornament. Village women, festivals, Radha and Krishna. The Lalit Kala Akademi gave him its National Award in 1956 and again in 1960.

He ran a private art school, the Nutan Kala Mandir, and in 1968 went back to the J. J. School as a teacher. His work travelled to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia and Ceylon. He died in December 1982.
